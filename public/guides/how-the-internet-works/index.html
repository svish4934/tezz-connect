What is happening behind the scenes?

The internet is a network of networks. Your home network connects to your provider, and your provider connects with other networks. Information can cross many different networks before reaching its destination.

Why does DNS matter?

People remember names such as tezzconnect.online. Computers use IP addresses to identify destinations on a network. The Domain Name System (DNS) helps translate a domain name into the address needed to find the service. Your device or network may already have a saved answer, so a fresh lookup is not always needed for every visit.

Why are there packets?

Network data is divided into smaller units called packets. Each packet carries information that helps networks deliver it. The receiving system processes the data and your browser assembles the resources into the page you see. Different kinds of traffic use different transport methods, so not every packet behaves in exactly the same way.

Does every request follow this exact route?

No. This is a simplified teaching model. DNS may be resolved earlier or by a different service, the router and modem may be one combined device, and content delivery networks or caches may serve a page from a nearby location. Real routes can change depending on the provider, destination and network conditions.

Try a real-world comparison

Open a page while connected to Wi-Fi, then try again using mobile data. The route and network equipment are different, but the basic idea—your device requests information and a service sends it back—remains the same.

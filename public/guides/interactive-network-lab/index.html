LEARN BY EXPERIMENTING

Interactive network lab

Change one condition at a time and see how it can affect a home network. These small experiments turn networking ideas into something you can explore.

TEZZ CONNECT · Hands-on learning · Updated 9 November 2026

How to use this lab

Move a slider, choose a scenario and read the explanation. The diagrams use simplified teaching models. They do not measure your actual Wi-Fi signal, internet speed or route to a server.

EXPERIMENT 01 · WI-FI BANDS

Which Wi-Fi band fits this room?

Distance, walls and device support all matter. Change the room conditions to compare the usual trade-offs between 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and 6 GHz.

2.4 GHzLonger reach

Often a useful starting point farther away or through walls.

5 GHzBalanced nearby

Often a strong choice for higher throughput at moderate range.

6 GHzShorter range

Can offer extra capacity nearby when both devices support it.

Nearby · Across home · Far room
Open space · Several walls
Suggested starting pointTry 5 GHz first

At a medium distance with one wall, 5 GHz is a reasonable first test if both devices support it. Compare results in the room you use.

Real performance also depends on router quality, channel congestion, antenna design, building materials and local regulations. A band label alone cannot predict your speed.
EXPERIMENT 02 · RESPONSIVENESS

What do ping, jitter and packet loss feel like?

Speed is not the whole story. Adjust these values to explore why a connection can feel delayed or inconsistent even when download speed looks good.

Connection behaviourResponsive

Low delay and little variation generally help interactive activities feel responsive.

Teaching illustration only — not a measured connection score
Ping usually reports the time for a request and reply to travel. Lower round-trip time generally feels more responsive.
Lower means response times vary less.
Lost packets may need retransmission or cause gaps.
These ranges and the meter are teaching illustrations, not a live measurement, benchmark or universal pass/fail score. Ping usually represents round-trip time. Server distance, game design, codecs, device performance and network routing also affect the experience.

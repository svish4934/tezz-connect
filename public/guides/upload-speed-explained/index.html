Upload speed explained: what is a good upload speed?

Upload speed affects how quickly your device sends information to the internet. It matters for video calls, sharing files, cloud backups and live streaming.

What is upload speed?

Upload speed tells you how quickly your device can send information to the internet. It is usually measured in Mbps (megabits per second).

Think of it this way: downloading is receiving a photo; uploading is sending that photo to someone else. Your internet plan may have different download and upload speeds, so it is worth checking both.

When will you notice upload speed?

  • Video calls: your camera and microphone send information to the other people on the call. A steady upload helps them see and hear you clearly.
  • Sending photos and videos: bigger files take longer to send if your upload speed is low.
  • Cloud backups: your phone or computer needs to upload files before they are safely stored online.
  • Live streaming: your device has to send video continuously while you broadcast.
  • Sharing your screen: your computer sends the screen image to everyone in the meeting.

For example, you might be able to watch a video without any trouble but struggle when you try to send a large video file. That can happen when your download speed is much higher than your upload speed.

Why might your upload speed be slow?

There can be more than one reason. Your plan may offer limited upload capacity. Someone at home may be backing up files. Your Wi-Fi signal may be weak, your router or device may be struggling, or your provider's network may be busy. The test server and route can also affect the result.

One slow test does not tell you which of these is the cause. Try a few checks before deciding that your plan needs an upgrade.

How to test upload speed fairly

  1. Pause cloud backups, file syncing and other large uploads if you can.
  2. Use the same device and test service each time you compare results.
  3. Try Wi-Fi near your router, then use Ethernet if it is available.
  4. Repeat at a different time and note whether other people were using the connection.
  5. Compare the result with the upload speed listed in your plan. Allow for normal network overhead and changes in conditions.

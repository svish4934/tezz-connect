PRACTICAL HOME NETWORK SECURITY

Check the security of your home Wi-Fi

Your router is the front door to your home network. A few careful settings can reduce avoidable risks and help you manage who can connect.

What these settings mean

Wi-Fi encryption and passwords

Encryption helps protect wireless traffic from people who are not authorised to join your network. Use the strongest mode supported by your router and devices. If WPA3 compatibility causes problems with an older device, check whether your router offers secure WPA2-AES rather than switching to obsolete encryption.

Administrator access and firmware

The administrator account controls router settings, so protect it separately from the Wi-Fi password. Firmware updates can include security fixes and stability improvements. Download updates only through the router's official interface or the manufacturer's trusted support channel.

Guest networks and connected devices

A guest network can keep visitors' devices separate from your main network, depending on how the router implements isolation. Some routers also offer a separate network for smart-home devices. Check the feature description rather than assuming every guest or IoT network provides the same separation.

Make changes safely

  1. Check the router model and read its official instructions before changing unfamiliar settings.
  2. Make one change at a time and confirm your devices can reconnect.
  3. Keep new Wi-Fi and administrator credentials somewhere secure.
  4. Do not disable the firewall or enable internet-facing administration to solve an ordinary connection problem.
  5. If the router is managed by your ISP, ask the provider about settings you cannot access.

Need help with Wi-Fi performance too?

Security and performance are different checks. If your connection is slow or unstable, use the connection troubleshooter and Wi-Fi improvement guide.

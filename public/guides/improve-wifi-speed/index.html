PRACTICAL WI-FI TROUBLESHOOTING

How to improve Wi-Fi speed and reliability

Before changing your internet plan, work through these checks to find out whether the issue is Wi-Fi coverage, interference, device load or the internet connection itself.

TEZZ CONNECT · Practical checklist · Updated 6 November 2026

Test your current speed

Quick checklist

  • Improve router placement
  • Choose the right Wi-Fi band
  • Check connected devices
  • Compare Wi-Fi and Ethernet

1. Move your router to a better spot

Start with something simple: put your router in an open, central place if possible. Avoid hiding it in a cupboard, putting it behind a TV or leaving it on the floor. Walls, metal objects and large appliances can weaken the signal.

If your home is spread across several floors, one router may not cover every room equally well. Try a few locations and test from the places where you actually use your phone or laptop.

2. Choose the right Wi-Fi band

Many routers offer more than one Wi-Fi band. You may see names such as 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz or 6 GHz in your Wi-Fi settings.

  • 2.4 GHz: often reaches farther and can work better through walls, but it may be crowded and usually has lower top speeds.
  • 5 GHz: can offer faster connections nearby, but the signal may weaken more through walls or over distance.
  • 6 GHz: can offer extra capacity on supported Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 devices, but both your router and device must support it.

There is no best band for every room. Choose the one that gives your device a steady connection where you use it.

3. Check what else is using your internet

One person watching a video, another downloading a game and a laptop backing up files can all use the same connection at once. If your Wi-Fi slows down at certain times, check whether another device is doing a large download or upload.

If your router has a connected-device list, look for devices you do not recognise or no longer use. Change your Wi-Fi password if you think someone unknown is using your network.

4. Reduce interference and keep equipment updated

Try not to place your router next to devices or objects that may interfere with its signal. In a crowded apartment building, nearby Wi-Fi networks can also compete for airtime. Some routers can choose a channel automatically; follow your router maker's instructions before changing advanced settings.

Check for router software (firmware) updates from the manufacturer. An update may fix bugs or security issues, but it will not necessarily increase your internet speed.

5. Compare Wi-Fi with a cable

This is one of the most useful checks. Run a speed test over Wi-Fi near your router. Then, if possible, connect the same device to the router with an Ethernet cable and test again.

If the wired result is much better, the issue may be your Wi-Fi signal, interference or router placement. If both results are similar and lower than expected, the cause may be somewhere else—such as your plan, provider network, device or test route.

    6. Test more than once

    Run a few tests at different times and write down where you were standing and whether you used Wi-Fi or a cable. Change one thing at a time, such as moving the router, so you can tell what actually helped.

    Before upgrading your plan

    A more expensive internet plan will not fix a weak Wi-Fi signal in a far-away room. First check whether the problem is Wi-Fi or the internet connection itself.

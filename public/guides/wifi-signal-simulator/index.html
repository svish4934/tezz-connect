Why does Wi-Fi get weaker?

Wi-Fi uses radio waves. As the signal travels through a home, distance and building materials can reduce the energy that reaches your device. Walls, floors, metal surfaces, appliances and the router's position all influence the result.

Does 2.4 GHz always reach farther?

Often, 2.4 GHz can travel farther and pass through some obstacles better than higher-frequency bands. But it can be more crowded and usually has less available channel capacity. 5 GHz and 6 GHz can provide faster links in suitable conditions, especially closer to the router, but their coverage can be more limited. Your device and router must support the band.

What should you do in a real home?

  1. Place the router in an open, raised and reasonably central location.
  2. Test from the rooms where you actually use the connection.
  3. Compare Wi-Fi with Ethernet near the router if possible.
  4. Change one thing at a time and repeat the test.
  5. Consider mesh or an access point only after checking placement and the wired connection.

Remember

A colourful signal map can explain a concept, but only a real test in your home can show actual performance. Use the simulator to learn what to investigate—not to decide that a particular room has a guaranteed speed.

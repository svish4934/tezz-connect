eSIM vs Physical SIM: Which Is Better for International Travel?

Compare eSIM and physical SIM cards for travel, including compatibility, activation, dual-SIM use, roaming charges and practical limitations.

By Suraj Vishwakarma · Published by TEZZ CONNECT

Choosing a SIM for an international trip is no longer limited to buying a small plastic card at an airport counter. Many travellers can now install a mobile plan digitally through an eSIM, keep their home number active and use a separate line for data. That convenience is useful, but it does not mean every eSIM is cheaper, every phone supports it, or every travel plan works in every country.

The practical choice is between two ways of storing and activating a mobile subscription: a removable physical SIM and a digital eSIM profile. The radio network, coverage, speed and billing are determined mainly by the operator and plan—not by whether the subscription is stored on plastic or downloaded to the phone.

This guide explains how both options work, what to check before departure, how to avoid accidental roaming charges, and how to choose a setup for a short holiday, a multi-country trip or a longer stay abroad. It includes India-specific operator considerations, but the compatibility checks apply to travellers from other countries too.

1. What is a physical SIM?

A physical SIM is the removable card inserted into a compatible SIM tray. It contains secure information used by the mobile network to identify and authenticate the subscription. The phone reads the SIM when it connects to the operator. A nano-SIM is the small format used by most modern smartphones that still have a physical SIM slot.

For travel, a physical SIM can be your existing home SIM, a local prepaid SIM purchased in the destination country, or a card issued by a travel connectivity provider. You may remove your home SIM and insert another card, or use both a physical SIM and a second supported SIM arrangement if your phone has dual-SIM capability.

The main advantage is familiarity. You can see and remove the card, move it to another compatible unlocked phone, and buy a local card from a shop where that option is available. The disadvantages are practical: you need a SIM tray, a tiny card can be misplaced, and swapping cards may interrupt access to your home number until you put it back.

2. What is an eSIM?

An eSIM is a digital SIM capability built into a compatible device. Instead of inserting a removable card, you download or activate a carrier profile using a supported method. Depending on the operator and device, that process may use a QR code, a carrier app, an activation notification, a device-to-device transfer or a carrier-assigned profile.

The embedded component is not itself a mobile plan. You still need a subscription from an operator or travel provider. A device can store multiple profiles, but the number that can be enabled at the same time depends on the hardware, software, region and carrier. A phone that can store several profiles does not necessarily allow all of them to be active simultaneously.

Because the profile is digital, you do not need to handle a physical card. That can be useful when buying a travel plan before departure or when moving between destinations. It also creates a different recovery consideration: deleting a profile may require the provider to issue a replacement or repeat its activation process. Do not delete a working eSIM simply to troubleshoot unless the provider tells you to.

4. Does eSIM provide better speed or coverage?

Not automatically. A mobile connection depends on the network available where you are standing, the bands supported by your phone, signal strength, network congestion, indoor coverage, the plan's speed policy and the operator's roaming agreements. An eSIM profile does not create additional radio capacity.

Two travel plans with similar names may use different wholesale partners or have different network priorities. One provider may allow access to several partner networks; another may restrict the profile to one network. Some plans may limit hotspot use or apply a fair-use policy after a certain amount of data. Those details matter more than the fact that the plan is digital.

When comparing providers, look for the actual network names in each destination, whether 4G/LTE or 5G is supported, whether speeds are reduced after an allowance, and whether tethering is permitted. A plan advertised as “5G ready” is not proof that 5G will be available at your hotel or on every partner network.

5. Check whether your exact phone supports eSIM

Do not rely only on a phone brand or family name. A model sold in one country can have different SIM hardware from a similarly named model sold elsewhere. Some variants support eSIM while others do not. Carrier-branded devices may also have restrictions.

Use this checklist before purchasing:

  • Find the complete model number in Settings, About phone or About device.
  • Check the manufacturer's official specifications for that exact regional model.
  • Look in the mobile network or SIM settings for an option such as Add eSIM, Add mobile plan or Download a SIM.
  • Ask the carrier or travel eSIM provider to confirm compatibility using the model number and, if requested, the EID.
  • Check whether the device is carrier-locked. A phone locked to one operator may reject a travel provider's profile even if the hardware supports eSIM.
  • Confirm that the phone supports the network bands and technologies used in the destination.

The EID is an identifier associated with the device's embedded SIM capability. Some providers request it during activation. It is different from the IMEI, which identifies the mobile device for network purposes. Share these identifiers only through the provider's verified activation process, not in public posts or messages to unknown sellers.

On iPhone, Apple's support guidance describes eSIM support on supported iPhone models and explains how to check carrier lock status. On Pixel devices, Google provides model- and operator-dependent eSIM guidance. Android menu labels can differ between manufacturers, so use the device maker's instructions rather than assuming every Android phone follows the same path.

6. Check whether the phone is unlocked

Hardware compatibility and network unlocking are separate checks. A phone may contain eSIM hardware but be restricted to the operator that sold it. If you plan to install a profile from a different provider, confirm that the device is unlocked before paying.

On supported iPhone models, open Settings, then General, then About, and look for the Carrier Lock entry. Apple's guidance says an unlocked iPhone displays “No SIM Restrictions”. For Android, the method varies by manufacturer and carrier. If the status is unclear, ask the original operator to confirm whether the device can accept another provider's SIM or eSIM.

Do not assume that a phone bought outright is automatically unlocked, or that a phone purchased abroad will work with every local network. Check both the lock status and radio-band compatibility. A phone may register on a network but lack support for a particular 5G band or voice technology.

“Travel eSIM” is a broad label. Providers package connectivity in different ways, and the product name does not tell you all the terms. Read the plan page and the activation instructions before purchase.

8. Data-only eSIM vs a plan with calls and SMS

Many travel eSIM products are data-only. They provide mobile internet but do not assign a conventional local phone number for cellular voice calls or SMS. You may still be able to use internet-based calling and messaging apps, but those services need data or Wi-Fi and may not replace every use of a normal phone number.

A local operator plan may include a local number, voice minutes and SMS, subject to local registration rules. A home-operator roaming pack may keep your usual number available for incoming calls, outgoing calls or text messages, depending on the pack. These are different products, so compare them based on what you actually need.

If you rely on bank verification messages, account recovery texts, work calls or delivery calls, confirm how your home number behaves while the travel line is active. Do not assume that an internet-only eSIM will receive SMS sent to your home number. Keep the home SIM or eSIM active if you need it, and check the roaming charges that may apply to calls, messages or background data.

9. Is an eSIM cheaper than international roaming?

Sometimes, but there is no universal answer. A travel eSIM may offer a fixed data allowance for a destination, while a home operator may sell a roaming pack that includes data, calls and SMS. A local SIM may be more economical for a longer stay, but could require identity verification, a local address or a shop visit.

Compare the full cost rather than the headline price:

  • Price including taxes and any activation fee.
  • Amount of high-speed data and what happens after it is used.
  • Validity period and when that period begins.
  • Whether the plan covers all countries on the itinerary.
  • Whether calls, SMS and a local number are included.
  • Whether hotspot or tethering is permitted.
  • Whether the plan can be topped up or extended.
  • Refund and cancellation rules if the phone is incompatible or the trip changes.
  • Support availability and the method for restoring a deleted or failed profile.

Calculate a rough cost per usable gigabyte only after checking the speed and fair-use conditions. A large allowance at a reduced speed may not meet the needs of a traveller who uses video calls or uploads large files. Conversely, a smaller allowance may be sufficient for maps, messaging and occasional browsing.

10. Can you keep your home number and use a travel eSIM?

On supported dual-SIM phones, you may be able to keep the home line active for calls or messages while using a travel eSIM for mobile data. The exact combination depends on the phone and carrier. Some devices support one physical SIM plus one eSIM; others support two active eSIMs; some allow several profiles to be stored but only a limited number to be active.

Before travel, decide which line should handle each task:

  • Mobile data: select the travel eSIM if it is the line intended for internet use.
  • Calls: choose the home line, travel line or Ask Every Time, depending on your needs.
  • SMS: keep the home number selected if you need messages sent to it.
  • Data switching: turn off automatic switching if the phone could move data to the home line and create roaming charges.
  • Roaming: check the roaming setting separately for each line.

Keeping a home line enabled does not guarantee that it is free to use abroad. Incoming or outgoing calls, SMS and cellular data may be billed according to the home operator's roaming terms. The safest configuration is to disable data roaming on the home line when you do not intend to use it, while following your operator's instructions for receiving essential messages.

11. How to install a travel eSIM before departure

Activation steps vary by provider, operating system and phone. The following is a planning sequence rather than a universal set of screen instructions.

  1. Confirm compatibility. Check the exact phone model, region, carrier lock and eSIM support.
  2. Choose the correct destination plan. Read the country list, network partners, data amount, validity and exclusions.
  3. Check the start rule. Determine whether validity begins at purchase, installation, activation or first connection to a supported network.
  4. Use a trusted connection. Install the profile using the provider's official app, QR code or activation flow while connected to reliable Wi-Fi.
  5. Label the line clearly. Use a name such as “Travel Data” so you can distinguish it from your home number.
  6. Set the preferred data line. Keep the home line as the default for calls or SMS if needed, and choose the travel profile for data when appropriate.
  7. Keep the instructions. Save the provider's support details and activation reference somewhere accessible without mobile data.
  8. Test at the right time. If the plan's validity starts on installation, do not install it too early. If it requires installation before departure, follow that rule.

Some providers issue a QR code that can be scanned only once. Others let you install the profile again or transfer it. Treat the activation instructions as account credentials: do not post the QR code publicly or forward it to an unverified person.

12. How to set up dual SIM on iPhone

On a compatible iPhone, the Cellular or Mobile Data settings let you manage the lines and select which one handles cellular data. The wording can differ by iOS version and region. Apple's international travel guidance explains how to use a home line and a travel eSIM together on supported devices.

  1. Install the travel eSIM using the provider's supported method.
  2. Open Settings and choose Cellular or Mobile Data.
  3. Confirm that both the home line and travel line are enabled if you intend to use both.
  4. Choose the line for cellular data and verify the displayed label.
  5. Review the default voice line and messaging preferences.
  6. Check Data Roaming for each line separately.
  7. Disable Allow Cellular Data Switching if you do not want the phone to use the other line for data automatically.
  8. When you arrive, check the network name and the travel provider's usage counter.

Do not copy instructions from a different iPhone generation without checking the current Apple guidance. eSIM support, the number of active lines and regional restrictions can differ by model and market.

13. How to set up dual SIM on Android

Android is used by many manufacturers, so menu names and supported combinations vary. On Google Pixel devices, the SIMs section under Network & Internet is used to add an eSIM and choose preferences for calls, texts and mobile data. Samsung, Motorola, Xiaomi and other manufacturers may use different menu labels.

  1. Open Settings and find SIMs, SIM Manager or Mobile Network.
  2. Choose Add eSIM or Download a SIM if the option is available.
  3. Follow the carrier's activation instructions.
  4. Return to the SIM settings and label the profile.
  5. Choose the travel profile for mobile data if that is the intended line.
  6. Set call and SMS preferences for the home line or travel line.
  7. Review data roaming and automatic data switching options for each line.
  8. Check the network name and data usage after registration.

Google's Pixel guidance states that dual-SIM capability depends on the phone model and operator. Other Android phones have their own hardware and carrier restrictions. If the Add eSIM option is missing, check the exact model's official support page and ask the operator before assuming the device can be upgraded through software.

14. India-specific eSIM considerations

In India, major operators publish their own eSIM eligibility and activation instructions. Support can vary by device, plan type, account status and activation method. Apple's carrier-support directory lists Airtel, Reliance Jio and Vi among Indian providers supporting eSIM activation methods on compatible iPhones, but that directory should not replace checking the operator's latest rules for your particular line.

Jio: Jio's official eSIM information describes converting an eligible existing number through the MyJio process. Its instructions include confirming the registered email address, providing the target device's EID and completing verification. Jio also publishes device compatibility and activation guidance. Check the current process before beginning because security and verification steps can change.

Airtel: Airtel's official eSIM page describes requesting a conversion through the Airtel app, selecting the device, providing the EID and completing OTP and call verification. The provider also publishes device setup instructions. Check the latest activation window and roaming support directly with Airtel.

Vi: Check Vi's official eSIM eligibility, supported device list and current activation method for your connection type. Do not assume that a procedure described for another operator will work on Vi.

For international travel, converting a home number to eSIM does not remove the need for an international roaming pack. A Jio, Airtel or Vi eSIM still follows that operator's roaming rules when it connects to a foreign partner network. A separate travel eSIM is a different subscription and may be data-only.

For a traveller who needs to preserve an Indian number for bank messages, work or family calls, plan the dual-SIM arrangement before leaving India. Confirm whether the home operator requires roaming activation in advance, whether incoming SMS is supported in the destination, and what charges may apply. Keep the operator's verified support contact available offline.

15. What if your travel eSIM connects to a different network?

Some travel plans use roaming agreements with local operators. The provider may specify one preferred network or list multiple supported networks. Your phone's automatic network selection may choose a partner based on availability and signal. If the plan requires a particular partner, connecting to another network could cause service failure or different billing.

Read the provider's network instructions before changing settings. If manual selection is recommended, open the mobile network settings, turn off automatic selection temporarily and choose a listed partner. If the connection does not work, restore automatic selection or contact support. Avoid selecting random networks simply because they appear in the list.

Network availability can also change between cities, buildings and transport routes. A plan that works at the airport may perform differently in a rural area or underground station. This is a coverage issue, not necessarily a fault with the eSIM profile.

16. Hotspot and tethering: check before you buy

Travellers sometimes expect a phone plan to share data with a laptop, tablet or another person's phone. Not every travel eSIM allows hotspot use, and some plans apply different restrictions to tethering. A plan can connect the phone to the internet while still limiting the ability to share that connection.

Check the plan terms for Personal Hotspot, tethering, device sharing and fair-use restrictions. If hotspot is allowed, remember that a laptop can use data quickly through cloud sync, operating-system updates, photo backup and video meetings. Set the laptop's connection as metered where possible and pause large downloads until you have reliable Wi-Fi.

Do not assume that a plan's “unlimited” wording means unlimited high-speed tethering. Read the speed policy, daily limits and acceptable-use terms. If you need to connect multiple devices for work, choose a plan that explicitly supports the expected usage.

17. Security and privacy: what changes with eSIM?

An eSIM profile cannot be removed by simply opening the SIM tray, which can make it harder for someone who steals a phone to remove the line immediately. That is a useful security characteristic, but it is not a substitute for a strong screen lock, account security or remote device protection. A stolen unlocked phone may still expose accounts and personal data.

Protect the email account used for eSIM activation. A provider may send a QR code, profile notification or account recovery link to that address. Use a strong password and multi-factor authentication where available. Do not share activation codes, one-time passwords, QR codes, EID or account details with unsolicited callers.

Physical SIM users can set a SIM PIN where supported. This adds a PIN requirement when the SIM is used in another device, but entering the wrong PIN repeatedly can lock the card and require a PUK code from the operator. Keep the PUK in a secure place and follow the carrier's instructions.

Neither SIM type makes browsing private by itself. A mobile operator can process connection metadata under its applicable policies and laws. Use secure websites, keep your phone updated and be cautious with unknown Wi-Fi networks regardless of which SIM format you choose.

18. What happens if the phone is lost or damaged?

If a phone with a physical SIM is lost, the card may be removed and used in another compatible phone unless it is protected. If a phone with an eSIM is lost, the profile remains associated with the device until the operator or account process disables or transfers it. In both cases, contact the operator promptly to suspend the service and protect your number.

Before a trip, record the official support channel and the steps required to block a lost SIM or eSIM. Make sure you can access your email and provider account from another device. Keep recovery codes and important account information securely, not in an unprotected note on the phone itself.

If the phone is damaged, a physical SIM may be moved to a replacement phone if it is compatible and unlocked. An eSIM generally needs a supported transfer or reissue process. Ask the provider what proof of identity is required and whether you can complete the transfer from abroad.

19. Troubleshooting: eSIM installed but no signal

An installed profile does not guarantee that the phone has registered successfully on a supported network. Use a methodical sequence rather than deleting and reinstalling the profile immediately.

  1. Confirm that the travel plan covers the country you are currently in.
  2. Check the plan's validity start time and expiry date.
  3. Confirm that the eSIM line is turned on in device settings.
  4. Check that the travel profile is selected for mobile data.
  5. Enable Data Roaming on the travel line if the provider's instructions require it.
  6. Check whether the plan requires a specific partner network.
  7. Toggle airplane mode briefly or restart the phone to refresh network registration.
  8. Check whether the phone is carrier-locked or missing required network-band support.
  9. Confirm that the provider's data allowance has not been exhausted.
  10. Contact the provider with the phone model, destination, local network name and any error message.

Keep the profile installed while troubleshooting unless the provider instructs you otherwise. Some QR codes are single-use, and deleting the profile may make recovery harder. If the provider asks you to reset network settings, understand that saved Wi-Fi networks and other preferences may be removed.

20. Troubleshooting: QR code will not scan or profile will not download

First check that the phone supports eSIM and is connected to stable Wi-Fi. Make sure the QR code is complete, readable and intended for that device. If the code is displayed on the same phone you are trying to configure, you may need to use the provider's manual activation method or display the code on another screen.

Some providers require the activation request to be completed in their app rather than by scanning a QR code. Others issue a profile that can be installed only once. Check whether the code has expired, whether the purchase is assigned to the correct email address, and whether the phone's software is up to date.

If the profile download stops, do not repeatedly scan or request replacement profiles without checking the provider's status. You could create duplicate requests or invalidate an existing activation. Contact official support and provide the order reference through a secure channel.

21. Troubleshooting: data is working but calls or SMS are not

This is common with data-only travel eSIMs. Internet access does not mean that the profile includes a telephone number, cellular voice or SMS. If you need ordinary calls or text messages, check the plan's product description and supported services.

If you are keeping your home line active, check that it is enabled and selected for the relevant call or SMS function. Confirm the roaming conditions with your home operator. Some services may depend on the visited network, voice-over-LTE roaming support or the phone's dual-SIM behaviour. Internet calling apps can work over the travel data connection, but they do not automatically provide SMS delivery to your home number.

22. Should you buy before departure or after landing?

Buying before departure can reduce the time spent looking for connectivity after landing. You can compare providers, read terms and install a profile while you still have access to your home connection. However, check when validity begins. If the plan starts counting days at installation, buying too early may waste part of the validity period.

Buying after arrival can be useful if you want to compare local operators in person or need a local number. It may also require finding a shop, completing identity checks and having a working connection for activation. Airport kiosks are convenient but may have limited choices or different prices from online offers.

A sensible approach is to shortlist options before travel, verify the exact plan and activation rule, and decide whether to install before departure or after arrival. Keep a backup connection plan, such as airport Wi-Fi or the home operator's emergency roaming option, in case activation is delayed.

24. A pre-travel checklist

  • Confirm the exact phone model supports the chosen SIM format.
  • Check whether the phone is unlocked.
  • Verify destination coverage and supported network partners.
  • Compare data, calls, SMS, hotspot and validity terms.
  • Check when the plan's validity begins.
  • Confirm whether the plan is data-only.
  • Decide which line will handle calls, messages and mobile data.
  • Disable data roaming on any line that should not use cellular data abroad.
  • Turn off automatic data switching if it could select the wrong line.
  • Save the activation instructions and support details offline.
  • Protect the account email and activation QR code.
  • Keep the home SIM or eSIM recovery instructions available.
  • Check the first data session and provider usage counter after arrival.

25. Returning home after using a travel eSIM

When you return, confirm that your home line is enabled and selected for mobile data. If you used a travel eSIM as a secondary line, turn it off when it is no longer needed. Do not delete it until you have checked whether the provider permits reuse or whether you may need it again on a later trip.

Check your home operator's network registration, data selection and roaming settings. If the phone has automatic line switching, review the setting so that the home connection behaves as expected. If you converted your home physical SIM to eSIM before travelling, confirm that the home profile remains installed and working.

Review the final roaming bill after the operator has processed delayed usage records. Keep the plan confirmation and usage screenshots until charges are clear. If a charge appears unexpected, ask the operator for the itemised usage record and the tariff applied.

Frequently asked questions

Is an eSIM better than a physical SIM for international travel?

An eSIM is convenient when the phone supports it and a suitable travel plan is available. A physical SIM may be better when the phone lacks eSIM support, a local operator sells a better visitor plan, or you need to move the card between devices.

Does eSIM give faster internet than a physical SIM?

No. Speed depends on the operator, network, signal, congestion, device and plan. The SIM format alone does not make the connection faster.

Can I use my Indian number and a travel eSIM together?

On a compatible dual-SIM phone, you may be able to keep your Indian line active while using a travel eSIM for data. Check the phone's supported active-SIM combination and disable data switching if it could use the home line unexpectedly.

Will a travel eSIM stop international roaming charges?

It can provide a separate data service, but it does not automatically stop charges on your home line. Your home SIM may still incur roaming charges for calls, SMS or data if it is used abroad.

Can I use WhatsApp with a data-only eSIM?

Messaging and internet calls can work over the travel eSIM's data connection. The app account may remain associated with your existing number, but ordinary cellular SMS and voice services are not included unless the plan says so.

Can I install an eSIM before travelling?

Often yes, but follow the provider's instructions and check when validity begins. Some plans start when installed; others start when they first connect to a supported network.

Can I transfer an eSIM to a new phone?

Sometimes. Transfer support depends on the device, operating system and carrier. Some providers require a fresh activation or reissue. Confirm the process before erasing or trading in the old phone.

Can I store multiple eSIMs on one phone?

Many compatible devices can store multiple profiles, but the number that can be active at once is limited and varies by model, region and operator. Check the manufacturer's official specifications.

Does a travel eSIM include a phone number?

Not always. Many travel eSIMs are data-only. Check whether the plan includes a local number, cellular voice and SMS if you need those services.

Can I use hotspot with an eSIM?

Only if the device and plan allow it. Check the provider's tethering policy, speed restrictions and fair-use conditions before relying on hotspot for a laptop or other devices.

What happens if I delete my eSIM?

The profile is removed from the phone and may stop working. You may need the provider to issue a replacement or authorise a new installation. Do not delete a profile as a first troubleshooting step.

Is eSIM safer if my phone is stolen?

An eSIM cannot be removed by taking out a card, which can make immediate SIM removal harder. However, you still need a screen lock, secure accounts and a way to suspend the service if the device is lost.

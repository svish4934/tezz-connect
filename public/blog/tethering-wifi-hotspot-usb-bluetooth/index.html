What Is Tethering? Hotspot vs USB vs Bluetooth

Tethering lets one device share its internet connection with another. Learn what it means and how Wi-Fi hotspot, USB tethering and Bluetooth tethering differ.

By Suraj Vishwakarma · Published by TEZZ CONNECT

Tethering means sharing one device’s internet connection with another device. For example, a phone can share its mobile data with a laptop using a Wi-Fi hotspot, a USB cable or Bluetooth. The best method depends on the devices, task and data plan.

Your laptop is ready for a video call, but the Wi-Fi has gone down. If your phone still has mobile data, you may be able to get the laptop online by sharing the phone’s connection. That is tethering. You can do it over Wi-Fi, USB or Bluetooth, and each method has a different trade-off.

First, what do “tethering” and “hotspot” mean?

Tethering is the broad term for letting one device use another device’s internet connection. A mobile hotspot is the familiar Wi-Fi version: your phone creates a small wireless network, and other devices join it with a password. People often use the two terms as if they mean exactly the same thing, but hotspot is just one way to tether.

Wi-Fi hotspot: easiest when more than one device needs access

Turn on the hotspot, find its network name on your laptop or tablet, and enter the password. There is no cable to find and several devices can usually connect. The downside is that the phone has to keep its Wi-Fi radio working while sharing mobile data. Video calls, laptop updates and cloud backups can drain the phone’s battery and use more data than you expected.

Before starting, check the hotspot password and look at your mobile plan. Some operators limit tethering or charge for it, even when ordinary phone data is included.

USB: useful for a one-computer connection

Connect the phone to a computer with a cable that supports data, then enable USB tethering in the phone’s settings. This avoids creating a Wi-Fi hotspot and can be handy if the computer’s wireless adapter is unavailable. A connected phone may charge, but do not count on it: power delivery depends on the cable, port and devices.

There is an important compatibility catch: Google says Android phones cannot provide USB tethering to Mac computers. Check the instructions for your own phone and computer before relying on this method.

How does Bluetooth tethering work?

Bluetooth tethering shares your phone’s mobile-data connection with a paired computer over Bluetooth. Pairing creates the connection between the devices; you must also turn on the phone’s Bluetooth tethering option before the computer can use the phone as an internet connection. The exact menu names vary by phone and computer.

In general, turn on Bluetooth on both devices, pair them, then open your phone’s hotspot or tethering settings and enable Bluetooth tethering. On the computer, choose the paired phone’s network or internet-access option if it is offered. If the phone is paired but the computer still has no internet, check that Bluetooth tethering is enabled on the phone and that your mobile plan allows it.

Bluetooth tethering is usually best for light browsing or messaging when Wi-Fi and USB are unavailable. It is generally slower than a Wi-Fi hotspot or USB connection, so it is not a good first choice for large downloads or high-quality video. Support and performance vary by device.

Which one should you use?

  • Wi-Fi hotspot: a good default for a laptop and other nearby devices.
  • USB: worth trying for one compatible computer, especially when you want a direct cable connection.
  • Bluetooth: a fallback for modest tasks when both devices support it.

Whichever method you choose, tethering does not create extra data. It simply moves your phone’s connection to another device. A laptop can quietly use a lot of it, so pause automatic updates and cloud syncing if your allowance is limited. Turn sharing off when you finish, and use a password rather than leaving a hotspot open.

Common tethering questions

What is the difference between tethering and a hotspot?

Tethering means sharing one device’s internet connection with another. A Wi-Fi hotspot is one type of tethering; USB and Bluetooth are other connection methods.

Does tethering use hotspot data?

Tethering uses the mobile data allowance on the phone that is sharing its connection. When you connect a laptop through a Wi-Fi hotspot, USB or Bluetooth, its internet activity is carried over that phone’s mobile connection. Your carrier’s plan may apply separate hotspot or tethering limits, so check its terms.

How does Bluetooth tethering work?

Pair the phone and the other device over Bluetooth, then enable Bluetooth tethering in the phone’s connection settings. The exact steps and compatibility vary by device. It is generally better suited to lighter internet use than large downloads.

Can I use USB tethering with any computer?

No. USB tethering depends on the phone, operating system, cable and computer compatibility. For example, Google says Android phones cannot provide USB tethering to Mac computers. Check the support instructions for both devices before relying on it.

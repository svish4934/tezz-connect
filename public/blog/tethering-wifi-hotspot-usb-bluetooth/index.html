What Is Tethering? Wi-Fi Hotspot vs USB vs Bluetooth

Learn what tethering means and compare Wi-Fi hotspot, USB and Bluetooth sharing for laptops, tablets and other devices.

Tethering lets one device share its internet connection with another. A phone can, for example, use its mobile data connection to bring a laptop online when fixed Wi-Fi is unavailable. The best method depends on how many devices you need to connect, whether you have a cable and how much battery you can spare.

Tethering and a mobile hotspot are related, but not identical

Tethering is the general practice of sharing an internet connection. A mobile hotspot is usually the Wi-Fi network created by a phone for nearby devices. In everyday conversation, people often use the terms interchangeably.

Wi-Fi hotspot: convenient for several devices

A phone creates a wireless network that laptops, tablets and other devices can join with a password. It is usually the easiest option when you need to connect more than one device or want to keep the phone nearby without a cable. The phone’s battery and mobile-data allowance can be used quickly, especially when a laptop starts cloud backups or large updates.

USB tethering: a direct cable connection

USB tethering connects a phone to one computer through a data-capable cable. It avoids a separate Wi-Fi link and can be useful when the computer’s wireless connection is unavailable. The phone may charge while connected, but charging behaviour depends on the cable, port and devices. Android USB tethering is not supported with Mac computers in Google’s current guidance.

Bluetooth tethering: a lower-bandwidth alternative

Bluetooth can share a connection after the devices are paired. It is generally suited to lighter tasks rather than large downloads or high-resolution streaming. Support and setup vary by operating system and device.

Check your plan, data use and security

Some mobile operators restrict tethering or charge extra. A plan described as unlimited may still have separate hotspot limits or speed rules. Use a strong hotspot password, connect only devices you trust and switch sharing off when finished. Keep an eye on laptop background updates, video streaming and cloud sync.

Which method should you choose?

Choose Wi-Fi hotspot for convenience and multiple devices, USB for a direct single-computer connection, and Bluetooth when supported and only modest data use is needed. None of these methods creates extra data allowance: the shared traffic is still subject to the source connection’s plan.

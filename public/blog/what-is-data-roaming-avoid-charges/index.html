Internet in Everyday Life

What Is Data Roaming and How Can You Avoid Unexpected Charges?

Find out how mobile data roaming works, why charges vary by destination and how to prepare your phone before travelling.

By Suraj Vishwakarma · Published by TEZZ CONNECT

Data roaming lets your phone use a mobile network outside the coverage area of your home operator, including a network in another country. It is useful when travelling, but the price and included allowance can be very different from your domestic plan. A phone can connect automatically, background apps can use data, and a travel pack may cover only selected countries or partner networks.

The safest way to avoid an unexpected bill is to prepare before departure: confirm the destination and partner network, check the exact pack and its validity, understand what happens when its allowance expires, and decide which SIM should carry mobile data. This guide explains the technology and gives you a practical checklist for travel from India or elsewhere.

1. What is data roaming?

Your home mobile operator may not own radio access networks in every country. Instead, it can have commercial roaming agreements with mobile operators in other destinations. When you travel, your SIM or eSIM can register on a permitted partner network. That visited network provides radio access and routes services according to the agreements and technical arrangements between the operators.

Your phone number and home subscription remain associated with your original provider. The partner network is the local network your device is using; it does not become your home operator. The roaming arrangement lets you continue to use services such as mobile data, calls and SMS, subject to plan coverage, device compatibility and local network availability.

Roaming is not the same as changing your phone's country setting, connecting to airport Wi-Fi or using a travel eSIM from another provider. A travel eSIM may connect directly to a local or regional service provider and can have a different billing arrangement. A hotel Wi-Fi connection uses a local wireless network and does not normally count as cellular roaming data.

2. Domestic data allowance usually does not travel with you

A domestic plan is designed around the operator's home service area and the plan terms. International roaming is often a separate service with its own rates, packs, destinations and validity. Do not assume that an unlimited domestic data benefit or a daily data quota will apply when your phone registers on a foreign network.

For example, your home plan might include a daily high-speed allowance in India. Once abroad, the operator may require an international roaming pack, charge standard roaming rates, or offer a destination-specific allowance. The exact result depends on the provider and the plan. Even a plan marketed as unlimited may have a separate roaming quota or a fair-use condition.

TRAI advises consumers to check the tariff applicable to the partner operator in the country being visited. International roaming prices are not one universal rate set by a single global tariff. The price can differ by destination, operator, service type and offer. See the TRAI Roaming FAQ.

3. Why roaming bills can rise unexpectedly

Unexpected charges are often caused by a mismatch between what the traveller believes is included and what the active plan actually covers. Common examples include:

  • No roaming pack: the SIM is allowed to roam and usage is billed at standard pay-as-you-go rates.
  • Wrong destination: the pack covers one list of countries, but the traveller visits a country outside that list.
  • Wrong partner network: the plan requires a designated network, while the phone registers on another available partner.
  • Pack expiry: the included validity ends while the phone is still using roaming services.
  • Allowance exhausted: the high-speed data quota or voice/SMS entitlement has been consumed.
  • Different service type: calls to a third country, premium numbers, satellite or maritime networks may not be included in ordinary travel benefits.
  • Background data: cloud backup, app updates, email sync, maps and video can consume data without a deliberate action.
  • Dual-SIM selection: the home SIM remains selected for mobile data even though a travel SIM was installed.
  • Border or transit connection: the phone may connect to a network in a neighbouring country or on a ship or aircraft, where different rates apply.

Do not rely on the signal bars or the country name displayed by a travel app as proof that your pack applies. Check the operator's welcome SMS, account app and the exact roaming pack details.

4. What to check in a roaming pack before buying

Compare the pack against your itinerary and usage, not just its headline price. A low-cost pack with a small data allowance may be suitable for maps and messaging but inadequate for remote work or video calls.

5. Understand the difference between a roaming service and a roaming pack

Operators may distinguish between enabling international roaming on the number and purchasing a pack that provides discounted or bundled usage. These are not necessarily the same action. A service activation can allow the SIM to register abroad, while a pack determines the included data, calls, SMS and validity.

Some operators bundle activation with pack purchase; others let a customer activate the service separately and use standard rates. The process can also differ between prepaid and postpaid accounts. Before travel, ask two separate questions: “Is international roaming enabled on my number?” and “Which pack or tariff will apply to each service in my destination?”

Do not assume that a pack purchase automatically means every roaming service is enabled, or that enabling roaming means your domestic allowance is included. Check the operator's confirmation and account status.

6. TRAI consumer protections and bill-shock alerts in India

TRAI's consumer information describes protections intended to reduce bill shock from international mobile roaming. It states that international roaming service is inactive by default for consumers and is activated on the consumer's request. Once activated, it can be deactivated at the consumer's request. TRAI also says providers must send information immediately after activation, including confirmation of activation and applicable one-time or recurring tariffs through SMS, email and the mobile application.

The TRAI Roaming FAQ describes alerts at charge thresholds such as 50%, 75% and 100%. These alerts are useful safeguards, but they are not a substitute for checking the pack terms, usage meter and account notifications. Message delivery may be delayed or a phone may not be connected to a partner network at the moment an alert is generated.

Read the current TRAI Roaming FAQ for the consumer guidance. For a billing dispute, first raise a complaint with your service provider and keep the docket number. Follow the operator's complaint and appellate process if the matter is not resolved. Keep evidence of the pack purchased, the activation confirmation, the destination, the usage alerts and the disputed charge.

7. Prepare your phone before leaving India

Do the important account and settings checks while you still have reliable access to your home network or Wi-Fi. Do not wait until you arrive at the airport or destination to discover that a pack cannot be purchased or activated.

  1. Confirm the destination. Check every country on your route, including transit and stopover countries.
  2. Check device compatibility. Confirm that your phone supports the bands and technologies used by the partner networks, and that it is not carrier-locked if you plan to use another SIM.
  3. Check the account. Verify that international roaming is enabled if required and that the correct number is covered.
  4. Choose the pack. Compare data, voice, SMS, validity, partner networks and post-quota rates.
  5. Check activation timing. Find out whether validity starts on purchase, a scheduled date or first eligible usage abroad.
  6. Save support details. Keep the operator's international customer-care number and the pack confirmation somewhere accessible offline.
  7. Update your phone. Install necessary operating-system and travel-app updates before departure over trusted Wi-Fi.
  8. Set data controls. Review background data, automatic updates, cloud backup and app data permissions.
  9. Plan for OTPs. Check whether your bank or essential service requires SMS to your home number, and understand whether incoming SMS is included or charged under your plan.
  10. Keep a backup connection. Consider a travel eSIM, local SIM or trusted Wi-Fi option if your main roaming service fails.

8. Android settings: control data roaming and background usage

Android menu names vary by manufacturer and software version. Common paths include Settings, Network & Internet or Connections, SIMs or Mobile Network, then Data Roaming. On some phones the setting is under a specific SIM's mobile-network options.

If you do not intend to use cellular data abroad, turn Data Roaming off before departure. You can also disable mobile data entirely or use airplane mode and turn Wi-Fi back on when needed. If you have bought a roaming pack and intend to use it, enable data roaming only for the SIM and service covered by the pack, following the operator's instructions.

Review app data usage and restrict background data for applications that do not need to update while travelling. Turn off automatic downloads and cloud photo backups over mobile data. Mark a laptop hotspot connection as metered where supported. Keep in mind that switching off data roaming may not disable voice calls or SMS roaming; check the operator's voice and messaging tariff separately.

Do not treat a generic Android menu path as universal. Use your phone manufacturer's support instructions if the setting is not where expected. A useful starting point is the Android Help Centre.

9. iPhone settings: dual SIM, data line and roaming

On an iPhone, open Settings and select Cellular or Mobile Data. If you use one SIM, open Cellular Data Options or Mobile Data Options and review Data Roaming. With Dual SIM or Dual eSIM, select the specific line and check its roaming setting. Menu labels can vary by iOS version and region.

Check which line is selected for Cellular Data. If you use a travel eSIM for data and your home number for calls or messages, make sure the travel line is selected for mobile data and understand whether the home line can still incur roaming charges. Apple's guidance notes that using a travel eSIM alongside a home line can still result in roaming charges on the home line, depending on the configuration and services used.

Apple's current guidance explains how to turn Data Roaming off, check cellular usage and manage travel eSIMs. See About cellular data roaming options and Use eSIM while travelling internationally.

10. Travel eSIM versus roaming on your home SIM

A travel eSIM can provide data through a local or international provider without replacing your physical SIM. It can be convenient for a multi-country trip, especially when the provider sells a regional package. However, the eSIM is not automatically cheaper or better in every destination. Compare its country coverage, data allowance, speed policy, validity, supported networks, hotspot rules and refund conditions.

Before buying, check whether your device is unlocked and supports the eSIM technology and network bands used at the destination. Some phones support multiple stored eSIM profiles but only a limited number of active lines at once. The exact capability depends on the model and region.

If you keep your home SIM active for bank OTPs or important calls, disable its mobile-data roaming unless you deliberately want it to use cellular data. Select the travel eSIM as the data line and turn off automatic data switching if it could move data back to the home SIM. Test the configuration before relying on it.

11. What happens on a cruise ship, ferry or aircraft?

Ships and aircraft can use specialised onboard connectivity systems. A mobile network available on a vessel may use satellite backhaul and can have a different tariff from the ordinary terrestrial network at the port or airport. An aircraft may also offer an in-flight cellular or Wi-Fi service under separate conditions.

Do not assume that a country-specific roaming pack covers maritime or in-flight networks. Some operators explicitly exclude satellite, maritime, aircraft or premium-number usage from standard roaming benefits, or charge extra when such services are permitted. The phone may connect to a ship's network while the vessel is still near land.

To avoid accidental connection, use airplane mode during flights as directed by the airline, then enable onboard Wi-Fi if permitted. On a cruise, ask the operator or ship for the cellular network name and pricing. If you do not intend to use the ship's cellular service, keep cellular data and data roaming disabled and use the ship's Wi-Fi under its terms.

12. Background apps that quietly consume roaming data

When a phone connects abroad, apps may resume activity that was paused at home. The biggest surprises often come from services that run without an obvious full-screen notification.

  • Photo and video backup: newly captured media may upload automatically.
  • Operating-system updates: a large update may download when the phone detects an available connection.
  • App updates: application stores may download updates automatically.
  • Cloud drives: documents, presentations and offline folders may synchronise.
  • Messaging apps: media auto-download can include large videos and photos.
  • Maps: map tiles, route previews and traffic information can use data.
  • Streaming: autoplay and high-quality video can use a significant amount of data.
  • Hotspot clients: laptops and tablets may download updates or synchronise files.

Before departure, set photo backup and app-store downloads to Wi-Fi only, disable media auto-download where appropriate and download offline maps over trusted Wi-Fi. On a laptop, set the phone hotspot as a metered connection and pause large updates. Check the phone's data-usage screen during the trip rather than waiting until the pack is exhausted.

13. How much data might you need while travelling?

There is no universal travel-data requirement. A traveller who mainly uses text messaging, maps and email may need much less data than someone who uploads video, attends long video meetings or streams entertainment every day. The best estimate comes from your own usage history and the activities you expect to perform.

Use the phone's cellular data counter as a guide, but compare it with the operator's roaming usage meter. Counters can reset on different dates or update at different times. Record the starting value when the pack activates and check it daily during the trip.

14. What to do if your phone connects to the wrong network

Some roaming packs specify preferred partner networks. Your phone may choose another available network automatically, especially when signal conditions change. If the operator's pack requires a designated partner, usage on a different network may be billed at standard rates.

  1. Open the operator's pack details and identify the permitted network names for the country.
  2. Check the network name shown in the phone's status area or cellular settings.
  3. If required by the operator, open Network Selection and switch from Automatic to Manual.
  4. Choose only the network specified by the provider's current instructions.
  5. Wait for registration and confirm that the operator app or welcome message recognises the correct roaming service.
  6. If registration fails, restore Automatic selection or follow the operator's support instructions rather than repeatedly selecting random networks.

Manual selection is not always required. Some operators direct customers to leave network selection on Automatic. Follow the instructions for your particular pack and destination.

15. Troubleshooting: no signal or no mobile data abroad

If the phone shows No Service, SOS only or a roaming network but cannot access the internet, work through a controlled checklist.

  1. Confirm the account is eligible. Check whether international roaming is active and whether the pack covers the country.
  2. Check pack validity. Confirm that the start time and expiry have not passed.
  3. Check the data allowance. The quota may be exhausted or the pack may include voice and SMS but no data.
  4. Check the partner network. Confirm that the device is registered on a permitted network where the pack requires one.
  5. Check Data Roaming. Enable it for the intended SIM if the operator's instructions require it.
  6. Check the selected data line. On dual-SIM phones, make sure the correct SIM or eSIM is selected for cellular data.
  7. Restart the radio connection. Toggle airplane mode briefly, then allow the phone to register again. Apple recommends this as one troubleshooting step for roaming connection issues.
  8. Check network mode. Use a supported automatic network mode. Older handsets may not support the bands or voice technology used by the partner network.
  9. Test another location. Coverage may be limited indoors, underground or outside the partner's service area.
  10. Contact the operator. Provide the country, local network name, phone model, pack and error message. Ask whether the account is provisioned correctly.

Avoid changing many settings at once. Record the original settings and change one item at a time so you can identify what resolved the problem. Do not reset network settings unless you understand that saved Wi-Fi networks and other network preferences may be removed.

16. What to do if you receive an unexpected roaming charge

Start by identifying the service, date, destination and network associated with the charge. Roaming records can sometimes arrive after the actual usage, so the billing date may not be the same as the date you used the service. Ask the operator for the detailed usage record and the tariff applied.

  1. Download or screenshot the bill and itemised usage details.
  2. Compare the charge time with your travel dates, pack validity and usage alerts.
  3. Check the visited country and partner network shown in the record.
  4. Compare the usage with the pack's included data, call and SMS limits.
  5. Check whether the pack had expired or a post-quota rule applied.
  6. Raise a complaint with the operator and request a written explanation of the tariff and relevant pack clause.
  7. Keep the complaint docket number, email response and supporting screenshots.
  8. If the response is unsatisfactory, follow the provider's appellate process described in its consumer grievance procedure.

Do not publish your full phone number, account number, passport details or unredacted bill in a public forum. Share only the information needed through the operator's verified support channels.

17. A simple arrival-day checklist

  • Read the roaming welcome SMS and confirm the country and network.
  • Open the operator app and verify the pack, balance and expiry time.
  • Confirm which SIM is selected for mobile data.
  • Check Data Roaming for the intended line.
  • Disable mobile data on any home SIM that should not use roaming data.
  • Turn off automatic data switching if it could use the wrong line.
  • Check that background backup and automatic downloads are paused.
  • Save the operator's support contact and pack confirmation offline.
  • Check data usage after the first short session to make sure the correct pack is being consumed.

If anything looks inconsistent, disable cellular data on the affected line while you contact the operator. This is safer than continuing to browse while uncertain about the tariff.

18. Before you return home

When your trip ends, check the phone's selected data line and roaming settings before leaving the destination. If you used a travel eSIM, confirm which line should remain active after arrival home. Turn off or remove travel profiles only when you no longer need them and have saved any information required to reactivate them.

Check whether your home SIM has returned to its normal network and whether mobile data is selected on the intended line. If your roaming pack has remaining validity, do not assume it will carry over to a future trip. Review the expiry conditions and any auto-renewal settings.

Keep the final usage summary and bill until you are satisfied that all charges are correct. If a delayed roaming charge appears later, compare the date of use and the operator's itemised record with your travel notes.

Frequently asked questions

What is data roaming in simple words?

Data roaming allows your phone to use a mobile network outside your home operator's normal coverage, often through a partner network in another country. Charges and allowances depend on your plan and destination.

Will my Indian daily data plan work abroad?

Do not assume it will. International roaming usually has separate packs, allowances and tariffs. Check the operator's exact destination and plan terms before using mobile data abroad.

Should I turn data roaming off when travelling?

If you do not intend to use cellular data abroad, turning Data Roaming off is a useful precaution. If you have purchased a roaming pack, enable it only for the intended SIM and follow the operator's instructions.

Can I receive bank OTP SMS while roaming?

It depends on your operator, plan, destination and roaming service status. Check incoming SMS conditions before travel and do not assume that receiving messages is always free or included.

Does airplane mode stop roaming charges?

Airplane mode disables the phone's cellular radio, which prevents ordinary cellular roaming while it remains enabled. You can often turn Wi-Fi back on separately. Follow airline instructions during flights and check the device status.

Is a travel eSIM cheaper than my operator's roaming pack?

It depends on destination, data allowance, validity, network coverage, hotspot support and whether you need your home number for calls or SMS. Compare the full terms rather than price alone.

Can I use WhatsApp or other messaging apps without roaming?

Messaging apps can use Wi-Fi or data from a travel SIM/eSIM. If they use the home SIM's cellular data abroad, roaming rules may apply. Check the selected data line and app settings.

Why did I get a roaming charge even though I did not open any apps?

Background synchronisation, notifications, app updates or another device connected through hotspot may have used data. Check itemised usage and app-level data counters, then ask the operator to identify the charged service.

What happens when my international roaming pack expires?

The operator may stop service, apply standard pay-as-you-go rates or move usage to another defined tariff or pass. Check the pack's post-expiry terms and disable cellular data if you do not want further usage.

Does a roaming pack cover cruise ships and aircraft?

Not necessarily. Maritime and in-flight networks may use specialised systems and separate tariffs. Check the pack exclusions and the ship or airline's connectivity information before connecting.

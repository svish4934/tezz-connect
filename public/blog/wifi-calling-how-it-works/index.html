Wi-Fi Calling: How It Works and When to Use It

Learn how carrier Wi-Fi Calling works, when it can help with weak mobile reception and what device and network support you need.

Wi-Fi Calling allows a supported mobile phone to make and receive ordinary carrier voice calls over a Wi-Fi connection when mobile reception is weak or unavailable. It uses your mobile number and carrier service; it is different from making a voice call inside a messaging app.

How Wi-Fi Calling works

When the feature is enabled and your carrier supports it, your phone can route a voice call through a suitable Wi-Fi connection to your mobile operator. The call is still associated with your mobile service and phone number. The carrier decides how the feature is provisioned and how calls are billed.

When it can be useful

It may help in buildings with thick walls, basements or locations where cellular reception is limited but reliable Wi-Fi is available. It can also be useful when travelling, but international calling rules and charges depend on your carrier and plan.

What you need

You generally need a compatible phone, current software, an operator that supports Wi-Fi Calling for your device and account, and a working Wi-Fi connection. Not every carrier, country, region or Wi-Fi network supports the feature.

How to enable it

On supported iPhones, look in Settings under Cellular or Mobile Data, select the relevant line if you use multiple SIMs, and open Wi-Fi Calling. On Android, the option is usually in the SIM or mobile-network settings, but the path varies by manufacturer and carrier. Follow the operator’s activation instructions if a setting is missing.

Emergency calls and location details

Emergency-call handling can differ by country and carrier. Your operator may ask you to register an address or provide location information. Do not assume that Wi-Fi Calling behaves identically to a normal cellular emergency call in every region; review your carrier’s guidance.

If calls do not connect

Check that the carrier supports the feature on your account, confirm it is enabled for the correct SIM, update your phone and test another reliable Wi-Fi network. Some public or managed networks block the traffic needed for Wi-Fi Calling. Contact your operator if the option remains unavailable.

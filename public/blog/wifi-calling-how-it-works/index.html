Wi-Fi Calling: How It Works and When to Use It

Learn how carrier Wi-Fi Calling works, when it can help with weak mobile reception and what device and network support you need.

There may be a room in your home where mobile reception drops to one bar, even though the Wi-Fi works perfectly. Wi-Fi Calling can help in that situation. It lets a supported phone route ordinary calls through a Wi-Fi connection to your mobile operator, rather than relying only on the nearby cellular signal.

It is still your mobile number and carrier service

Wi-Fi Calling is not the same as calling through a messaging app. When your operator supports the feature and it is enabled for your line, calls use your regular mobile number. The operator handles the service, so availability, billing and the way calls move between Wi-Fi and cellular networks depend on that operator and your plan.

When it can make a difference

It is worth trying in places where cellular reception is weak but the Wi-Fi connection is stable: a basement office, a building with thick walls or a room far from a window. It will not fix a poor broadband connection. If the Wi-Fi itself is dropping or heavily congested, call quality may still suffer.

What needs to be supported?

You generally need a compatible phone, up-to-date software, a carrier that offers Wi-Fi Calling for your device and account, and a Wi-Fi network that allows the service. Support can vary by country, operator, phone model and even the specific mobile plan. If the setting is missing, it may be a carrier eligibility issue rather than a fault with the phone.

How to turn it on

On an iPhone, open Settings, choose Cellular or Mobile Data, select the relevant line if you use more than one SIM, then look for Wi-Fi Calling. On Android, the setting is commonly found under SIM or mobile-network settings, but the route differs by manufacturer. Follow your carrier’s activation steps if it asks you to register an emergency address.

Do not overlook emergency calling

Emergency-call handling is important and can differ by country and carrier. Your operator may ask you to confirm an address so emergency services have location information if you call over Wi-Fi. Do not assume that Wi-Fi Calling works identically to a normal cellular emergency call everywhere. Read the local carrier guidance and keep your address details current if registration is required.

If it does not work

First check that the feature is available on your plan and enabled for the correct SIM. Then update the phone, restart it and try another trusted Wi-Fi network. Some managed or public networks block the connections Wi-Fi Calling needs. If the option still does not appear, your mobile operator is the right place to confirm eligibility.

Why Does My Internet Slow Down at Night?

Evening slowdowns can have several causes. Compare wired and wireless performance before assuming your ISP is congested.

Internet performance is affected by several connected parts of your home network and the wider internet. This guide explains what to check and how to narrow down the cause without assuming that one test or device tells the whole story.

Why evenings can feel slower

More people may use the internet at home during evening hours, streaming video, gaming, joining calls and downloading files. This increase in demand can affect shared capacity in a home, building, access network or upstream route. However, a slowdown at night is not proof by itself that the ISP is oversubscribed.

Separate home congestion from provider congestion

A family member starting a large download can affect other users, especially when upload capacity is limited. Wi-Fi airtime may also become crowded as neighbours return home and use nearby networks. Provider-side congestion is another possibility, but it should be investigated after checking local equipment and usage.

Run a consistent comparison

Test at a quiet time and again during the slowdown, using the same device, test server and connection method where possible. Repeat each test rather than relying on one result. If you can, use Ethernet to bypass Wi-Fi. Record download, upload, latency and the exact time.

Check background activity and Wi-Fi

Look for cloud backups, operating-system updates, game downloads, security cameras and streaming devices. Check the router’s connected-device list. Place the router in an open central position and use a suitable Wi-Fi band. A strong signal does not rule out interference or wireless congestion.

Look for a wider pattern

If wired performance also drops at similar times over several days, and multiple devices are affected, the evidence points away from a single device. Ask neighbours using the same provider whether they notice a similar pattern, while remembering that different plans and network segments may behave differently.

When to contact your ISP

Share repeated time-stamped wired results, your plan details, location at an appropriate level and a description of affected services. Ask the provider to check line quality, outages, signal levels and local network congestion. Keep the complaint reference and follow the provider’s escalation process.

The key point

Evening slowdowns may involve household usage, Wi-Fi conditions, provider capacity or routing. A repeatable test pattern is more useful than assuming one cause from the time of day alone.

Before you finish

Change one variable at a time, record what you observe and repeat tests under comparable conditions. This makes it easier to identify a genuine pattern and explain the issue to your equipment manufacturer or internet provider.

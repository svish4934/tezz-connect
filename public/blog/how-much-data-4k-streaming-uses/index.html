Internet in Everyday Life

How Much Data Does 4K Streaming Actually Use?

Estimate how much data 4K video can consume, why usage differs by streaming service and how to manage playback quality.

Watching a film in 4K can use a surprising amount of data, especially if several people in the house stream every day. But there is no universal “4K uses this many gigabytes” answer. Streaming services encode video differently, and the amount sent to your screen can change while you watch.

Start with a real service example

Netflix says its High data-usage setting can use up to 7 GB per hour for Ultra HD. That is a useful planning figure if you use that service and setting, but it is not a promise that every 4K stream on every platform will use the same amount.

What does that mean over a month?

Using Netflix’s published upper figure as a simple example, a two-hour film could use up to about 14 GB. If you watched two hours every day for 30 days at that rate, the total would be about 420 GB. Treat that as an illustration, not a prediction: actual use may be lower when the service sends a lower bitrate or changes quality during playback.

Why the same resolution can use different amounts

Resolution tells you how many pixels make up the picture; it does not tell you the exact amount of data needed to deliver it. Fast-moving sport, dark scenes, animation and detailed landscapes can place different demands on video compression. Services also choose different encoders, bitrates and adaptive-streaming rules. The app may raise or lower quality as your connection changes.

Your TV’s 4K setting is not the whole story

A television can output a 4K picture even when the source video is lower resolution and being upscaled. To receive a native 4K stream, the title, subscription, device, app and connection all need to support it. Some services also have separate data-usage or playback-quality settings.

How to keep data use under control

  • Check the quality or data-use setting in the streaming service you actually watch.
  • Use a lower setting when streaming over a capped mobile connection.
  • Download shows over a suitable connection when the service allows offline viewing.
  • Include every TV, tablet and phone in your household estimate.

If your broadband plan has a monthly data cap, remember that streaming is only one part of the total. Game downloads, operating-system updates, photo backups and video calls can add to the same allowance.

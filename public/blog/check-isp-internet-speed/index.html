Broadband & ISP Insights

How Can I Check If My ISP Is Delivering the Speed I Pay For?

Use repeatable tests and your plan terms to assess performance; one speed-test result cannot prove whether a provider is meeting its commitment.

Read the plan terms

Check the speed tier and whether the rate is described as typical, maximum or otherwise qualified. Note any fair-use policy, data cap or traffic-management condition. Keep a copy of the plan confirmation and current terms so you compare results with the correct service.

Prepare a fair test

Use a computer connected to the router by Ethernet where possible. If testing Wi-Fi, stay close to the router and record that fact. Pause streaming, cloud backups, downloads and other heavy activity. Check that the Ethernet port and cable support the speed you are testing; older hardware can become a bottleneck.

Run repeated measurements

Use a reputable speed-test service and, if possible, the provider’s recommended test method. Run several tests at different times over multiple days. Record date, time, server, download, upload and latency. Avoid selecting only the best or worst result.

Separate home equipment from the provider network

Compare wired and wireless results. If wired results are consistently better, investigate Wi-Fi. If wired results are also poor, check cables, router or ONT indicators and whether other devices are using the connection. The provider may need to inspect line quality, signal levels, outages or network congestion.

Understand regulatory benchmarks carefully

TRAI’s broadband FAQ says providers must declare typical download and upload speeds in tariff offerings, and that at least 90% of measured test samples must meet or exceed those declared typical speeds. This is a provider-level measurement requirement, not a promise that every individual household test will match the stated rate at every moment. Read the applicable regulation, provider disclosures and your plan terms together; do not treat one measurement as a regulatory finding.

Raise a documented complaint

Send the provider your plan details and a table of repeated test results, including wired tests and timestamps. Ask for a fault investigation and a complaint reference number. Follow the provider’s published escalation process if the issue is not resolved, and retain all responses.

What makes a strong assessment

A fair assessment uses the right plan terms, a capable wired test setup, repeated measurements and a documented complaint trail. This is stronger evidence than comparing one Wi-Fi result with an advertised headline number.

How to Share Your Wi-Fi Password Using a QR Code on Android and iPhone

Share a home Wi-Fi connection more easily with built-in QR and password-sharing features on supported Android and Apple devices.

Having guests over and tired of reading out a long Wi-Fi password? On many recent phones, you can share access with a QR code or a built-in password-sharing prompt. The steps are different on Android and Apple devices, so here is what to look for—and what not to assume.

On Android: display the network’s QR code

On many Android phones, open Settings and go to Network & internet or Connections, then open the Wi-Fi network you are currently using. Look for Share. After you verify your identity, the phone can display a QR code that another device can scan. The exact labels and location vary by manufacturer and Android version.

Google’s Android instructions describe this built-in sharing flow. If you cannot find it, search your phone’s settings for “Share Wi-Fi” or check the manufacturer’s support page for your model.

On iPhone: use password sharing or a network QR code

Apple devices can share a saved Wi-Fi password with a nearby person when the devices meet Apple’s requirements—for example, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are on, both people are signed in and their contact details are saved. When the prompt appears, tap Share Password.

On supported current iPhone software, you can also open the saved network’s details and choose Show Network QR Code. Your guest can scan the code to join. If you do not see that option, use Apple’s nearby password-sharing prompt or share the password another way.

What does the QR code reveal?

A Wi-Fi joining code contains the network details a compatible device needs to connect. Treat it as a password, not as a harmless picture. Anyone who can scan a valid code may be able to join that network, depending on its security settings.

Share access without exposing your main network

If visitors come often, a guest Wi-Fi network is usually a better choice than giving everyone the credentials for the network used by your computers, storage devices and smart-home equipment. Keep the guest password private, change it if it has been shared too widely and avoid posting the QR code in a public place.

Do you need an online QR-code generator?

Usually not. The built-in phone feature is simpler and avoids typing your Wi-Fi name and password into an unfamiliar website. If you choose a third-party tool, understand whether the credentials are sent to or stored by that service before entering them.

If scanning does not work

Check that the code is fully visible, the guest is scanning the right network and the password has not changed. Some camera apps show a prompt or network details rather than joining automatically. The guest may need to tap the prompt to finish connecting.

What Happens When You Turn On Airplane Mode?

Understand what airplane mode switches off, why Wi-Fi or Bluetooth may still work, and what to check before flying.

Airplane mode is easy to misunderstand. The name suggests that your phone switches off completely, but that is not what happens. It changes which wireless connections the phone uses, especially its connection to the mobile network. Your screen, downloaded files, camera, alarm and many offline apps can still work.

What changes when you switch it on?

On most phones, turning on airplane mode initially disables the cellular connection. That means the phone stops using the mobile network for ordinary calls, texts and mobile data. The exact behaviour of Wi-Fi and Bluetooth depends on the device and its settings. Some phones turn them off at first; you may then be able to switch them back on separately.

Can you still use Wi-Fi on a plane?

Sometimes. If the airline offers in-flight Wi-Fi and permits passengers to use it, you can usually enable Wi-Fi while leaving airplane mode on. The phone’s setting does not replace the airline’s instructions. Follow the crew’s directions about when devices can be used and which connections are allowed.

What about wireless headphones?

Bluetooth headphones may continue to work or be switched back on, depending on your phone. Apple’s current guidance says Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can be used in airplane mode when the airline allows it. Android menus and behaviour differ by manufacturer, so check your own phone rather than assuming every model behaves the same way.

Does it stop roaming charges?

Keeping the cellular connection off can help prevent your phone from using a foreign mobile network. But airplane mode is not a complete travel-billing strategy. If you turn cellular service back on, use another SIM, or change data settings, charges may still apply. Check your operator’s roaming terms and the active SIM before travelling.

A simple pre-flight routine

  1. Check the airline’s device and connectivity instructions.
  2. Turn on airplane mode when instructed.
  3. Only re-enable Wi-Fi or Bluetooth if the airline permits it.
  4. If avoiding roaming is important, confirm that cellular data and the intended SIM settings are correct.

One last detail: airplane mode can reduce battery use when the phone would otherwise keep searching for a mobile signal, but the saving depends on the situation. It is a radio-setting, not a magic battery-saving mode.

Internet in Everyday Life

What Happens When You Turn On Airplane Mode?

Understand what airplane mode switches off, why Wi-Fi or Bluetooth may still work, and what to check before flying.

By Suraj Vishwakarma · Published by TEZZ CONNECT

Airplane mode is one of the most familiar phone settings, but it is also one of the easiest to misunderstand. The name can make it sound as if the phone shuts down or loses every wireless feature. In reality, airplane mode changes how the device uses its radios. Its main purpose is to stop ordinary cellular transmission while you follow the airline's instructions for portable electronic devices.

Your phone does not become useless when airplane mode is enabled. You can usually read downloaded documents, take photos, play offline music, use an alarm, write notes and run apps that do not need a network connection. Depending on the phone and airline rules, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth may also be available. The exact behaviour varies across iPhone and Android models, software versions and settings.

This guide explains what the setting does, why Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can sometimes remain available, what happens to calls and messages, how it affects roaming and battery use, and what to check before and during a flight. The key rule is simple: device settings never override the airline crew's instructions or the applicable aviation rules.

1. What is airplane mode?

Airplane mode is a device setting that disables or restricts selected wireless transmissions. Its central function on a mobile phone is to turn off the cellular connection so the phone does not attempt to communicate with nearby mobile towers in the normal way. Depending on the operating system, it may also initially switch off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

It is a radio-control setting, not a power-off command. The phone remains switched on, the display continues to work, and local features remain available. A camera does not need a mobile network to take a photograph. A downloaded film does not need cellular data to play. A note saved on the device can still be edited while the phone is offline.

Airplane mode is also not the same as turning off mobile data. Turning off mobile data generally stops internet access over the cellular connection, but the phone may remain registered with the mobile network for voice calls, SMS, signalling and other carrier services. Airplane mode is intended to disable the cellular radio connection more broadly.

2. What happens when you turn airplane mode on?

On most smartphones, enabling airplane mode immediately changes the status of the cellular connection. The phone disconnects from its normal mobile network registration and stops using cellular service for ordinary calls, SMS and mobile data while the mode remains active. The status bar may show an airplane icon instead of the usual cellular signal indicator.

What happens to other radios depends on the device. On many Android phones, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth initially turn off when airplane mode is enabled. Google Pixel support explains that users can turn them back on separately and that the phone can remember the choice for the next time airplane mode is used. Apple's support guidance similarly explains that Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can be used in airplane mode when the airline permits it.

Some devices remember the last Wi-Fi or Bluetooth state, so the next activation may not look exactly like the first. This is why two phones can display airplane mode while showing different Wi-Fi or Bluetooth icons. Do not assume that the same tap sequence produces identical radio states on every device.

For the safest interpretation, look at each connection indicator separately. The airplane icon tells you that airplane mode is enabled; it does not, by itself, prove that every other wireless function is off.

3. What happens to mobile calls?

Ordinary calls through your mobile operator generally cannot connect while the cellular radio is disabled. The phone is not using the normal cellular network to register for service, so incoming calls to the mobile number may go to voicemail, receive an unavailable message or be handled according to the carrier's network settings. The exact caller experience depends on the operator and any call-forwarding features configured on the account.

Airplane mode does not necessarily prevent every kind of voice conversation. If you are allowed to use an onboard Wi-Fi network and your service supports an internet-based calling feature, an app call may work over that Wi-Fi connection. Wi-Fi Calling provided by a mobile operator may also be possible in some circumstances, but availability, emergency-calling support and billing depend on the carrier, country and plan.

Do not assume that Wi-Fi Calling is free or that it will be treated like an ordinary local call. Ask your operator how Wi-Fi Calling is billed when connected to an overseas or aircraft Wi-Fi network. If the airline prohibits calls, do not place them even if the technology permits a connection.

4. What happens to SMS and OTP messages?

Traditional SMS messages are delivered through mobile operator infrastructure. With the cellular connection disabled, a phone normally cannot receive ordinary SMS over its home mobile network. This can matter to travellers who rely on bank verification codes, account recovery messages or work alerts.

Some services deliver verification codes through app notifications, email or internet messaging instead of SMS. Those methods may work if the phone has an approved Wi-Fi connection. However, an app notification is not the same thing as an SMS arriving at the mobile number.

Before flying, complete essential account verification and download any boarding passes, travel documents and tickets you may need offline. If you expect an important SMS during a trip, check whether the operator and airline allow the relevant connectivity and whether the phone can use an approved network. Do not disable airplane mode in flight just to check for a message unless the crew has said cellular service may be used.

5. Does mobile data stop working?

Mobile data over the cellular network is unavailable while airplane mode has disabled the cellular radio. Apps that depend on your operator's 4G or 5G connection will not be able to use that route. This includes web browsing, map updates, cloud synchronisation and streaming when no other internet connection is available.

If you turn Wi-Fi on while airplane mode remains enabled and connect to an approved network, internet access may work again. In that case, the data travels over Wi-Fi rather than your mobile operator's cellular data connection. Apps may behave as normal, although the onboard network may have a login page, speed limits, usage limits or restrictions on certain services.

Remember that Wi-Fi internet access does not automatically reactivate your mobile data plan. It is a separate network path. Conversely, if you turn airplane mode off, cellular service may reconnect and apps may resume background activity. If you are travelling internationally, that can have billing consequences depending on your SIM and roaming settings.

6. Can you use Wi-Fi while airplane mode is on?

Often yes, when the airline permits it. Many modern devices allow Wi-Fi to be enabled separately after airplane mode has been switched on. You can then connect to an aircraft's approved Wi-Fi service, if available. The airline may provide free messaging, paid internet access or no onboard connectivity at all.

On a Google Pixel phone, Google's instructions describe turning airplane mode on and then enabling Wi-Fi from the Internet settings or Quick Settings panel. On iPhone, Apple explains that Wi-Fi can be switched back on while airplane mode remains active. Menu names and remembered settings can vary with software updates.

Connecting to aircraft Wi-Fi may require accepting terms, entering a booking code or signing in through a captive portal. Some networks allow messaging but block voice and video calls. Others offer paid browsing packages. Follow the provider's terms and the crew's announcements.

Wi-Fi availability does not mean you can connect to any network you see. Use the airline's official network name and avoid lookalike access points. If a login page asks for payment details, verify that it belongs to the airline or its named connectivity provider.

7. Can you use Bluetooth headphones?

Bluetooth headphones and earbuds can often be used in airplane mode if the airline permits them. Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology, separate from the phone's connection to a mobile tower. Many phones let you turn Bluetooth back on while keeping cellular service disabled.

On some Android devices, Bluetooth is initially switched off with airplane mode and can then be re-enabled. Google Pixel support documents this behaviour and says the phone can remember the wireless setting. Apple's guidance also allows Bluetooth use in airplane mode when permitted by the airline.

Pair your headphones before boarding if possible. Download music, podcasts or films in advance so entertainment does not depend on aircraft Wi-Fi. Keep a wired alternative if your headphones run out of battery or the airline's entertainment system requires a cable. Follow instructions during taxi, take-off and landing; individual airlines may impose additional restrictions on accessories or device use.

8. What happens to GPS and location services?

Airplane mode is not the same as disabling every location feature. A phone may still be able to calculate its position using satellite navigation signals, depending on the device and its software. However, location accuracy, speed and app functionality can change when cellular and Wi-Fi assistance are unavailable.

GPS or other satellite positioning does not itself provide internet access. A map app may know where you are but still be unable to download new map tiles, search results or live traffic information without a data connection. Download offline maps before departure if you expect to need navigation without connectivity.

Some apps use a combination of satellite positioning, nearby Wi-Fi networks, mobile network information, device sensors and cached data. With airplane mode on, some of those inputs may be unavailable. Location behaviour is therefore not identical across phones, apps or environments.

Do not rely on a phone's location display as a substitute for aircraft navigation or safety equipment. Follow the airline's instructions and use approved onboard information systems for flight-related details.

9. What about NFC, contactless payments and other features?

Airplane mode does not necessarily disable every short-range or local feature. NFC, which is used for some contactless payments and device interactions, may remain available on certain phones, but its behaviour depends on the device, wallet software and local rules. Some payment functions also require a secure element or previously provisioned card data, while other features may need an internet connection.

Do not assume that every contactless payment will work offline. A payment terminal, card issuer or wallet may require connectivity or additional verification. Carry a physical payment alternative and check that travel cards or tickets are stored in a form that can be accessed without a live connection.

Other local functions—such as alarms, timers, calculator, camera, downloaded files and offline games—usually continue to work because they do not need the cellular network. Connected watches and accessories may continue working over Bluetooth if that radio is enabled.

10. Does airplane mode save battery?

It can, especially in places where the phone would otherwise keep searching for a weak or unavailable mobile signal. Cellular network searching and maintaining radio connections use power. Turning off the cellular connection can reduce that activity.

The amount of battery saved depends on what the phone is doing. If you keep Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, GPS-related services, a bright screen and demanding apps active, the overall saving may be modest. If the phone is idle with its cellular radio disabled, the reduction may be more noticeable in a poor-signal environment.

Airplane mode is not a replacement for battery saver or good battery habits. Reduce screen brightness, close power-hungry tasks when appropriate, download content before travel and use a suitable power bank within the airline's battery rules. Do not place a phone or battery pack in checked baggage if the airline's rules require it to be carried in the cabin.

11. Does airplane mode prevent international roaming charges?

Keeping the cellular connection off prevents ordinary cellular roaming while airplane mode remains active. That can be useful when you want to avoid a phone registering on a foreign terrestrial network or a special onboard mobile network. However, airplane mode is not a complete guarantee against every possible charge.

If you turn cellular service back on, the home SIM may connect to a roaming partner. A separate travel eSIM, a second SIM or an automatic data-switching feature can also change which line is used. Wi-Fi Calling and premium connectivity services may have their own terms. Check the operator's roaming policy, the active data line and the phone's per-SIM settings.

For a simple precaution, keep airplane mode enabled when you do not need cellular service, and use Wi-Fi only if the airline permits it. If you need your home number active after landing, review the data-roaming setting and select the intended SIM before enabling cellular service. For a more detailed billing checklist, see our guide to international data roaming and unexpected charges.

12. Why airlines ask passengers to use airplane mode

Aircraft are designed and certified with protection against interference from portable electronic devices, but airlines and aviation authorities still set conditions for their use. A phone transmitting to a terrestrial cellular network is a different situation from a device with its cellular radio disabled and only an approved Wi-Fi or Bluetooth connection operating.

Airlines may require airplane mode because portable devices can transmit radio signals, and the aircraft environment includes communication, navigation and other electronic systems that must operate reliably. The exact requirements can vary by aircraft type, airline, route, regulator and the service being used onboard.

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency explains that portable electronic devices may be used during much of a flight in many circumstances, but exceptions can apply, including during take-off and landing. It also notes that airlines commonly require airplane mode. This is not permission to ignore a crew instruction: the airline is responsible for the rules applied to that flight.

There is no safe passenger-side test for deciding whether a particular device transmission could affect a particular aircraft. Do not try to prove that airplane mode is unnecessary. Follow the cabin crew's instructions even if another airline or a previous flight allowed a different configuration.

13. Can you make calls on an aircraft Wi-Fi network?

Technically, some internet connections can carry voice and video calls. But technical capability does not mean the airline permits those calls. Aircraft Wi-Fi providers and airlines may block voice services or ask passengers not to make calls to preserve cabin comfort and avoid disturbing other travellers.

Internet messaging, email and browsing may be permitted while voice and video calls are restricted. The policy can also differ by route or service package. Check the airline's onboard Wi-Fi terms before boarding and follow any announcement from the crew.

Wi-Fi Calling from your mobile operator is a separate service from an app call. It may route calls through an internet connection, but whether it works in flight and how it is billed depends on the carrier and airline. Do not assume that enabling Wi-Fi Calling is allowed simply because Wi-Fi is available.

14. Airplane mode on Android: how to use it

Android devices share a general concept, but their menu layouts differ. On Google Pixel, the standard path is Settings, Network & internet, then Airplane mode. You can also open Quick Settings by swiping down from the top of the screen and tapping the airplane icon. Samsung, Motorola, OnePlus and other manufacturers may use a different menu structure or wording.

To use Wi-Fi or Bluetooth while airplane mode remains on, follow your device's instructions:

  1. Open Quick Settings or the Settings app.
  2. Turn on Airplane mode.
  3. Check whether Wi-Fi and Bluetooth have also turned off.
  4. If the airline permits them, turn on the required connection separately.
  5. Confirm that the cellular network remains disconnected.
  6. Before turning airplane mode off, check which SIM and mobile data settings will become active again.

Google Pixel support notes that the phone can remember whether Wi-Fi and Bluetooth were enabled during the previous airplane-mode session. This can explain why the same device may preserve those connections the next time you turn the mode on. Other Android models may behave differently.

15. Airplane mode on iPhone: how to use it

On a supported iPhone, you can turn airplane mode on from Control Centre or from Settings. The airplane icon appears in the status area. Apple explains that Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can be used in airplane mode if the airline allows them.

  1. Open Control Centre.
  2. Tap the Airplane Mode control.
  3. Check the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth indicators.
  4. If permitted, turn Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on separately.
  5. Keep the cellular connection disabled unless the airline and applicable rules explicitly permit another configuration.
  6. When the flight is over, turn airplane mode off when instructed and verify the preferred mobile data line.

Apple's current guidance also notes that the device may remember a Wi-Fi or Bluetooth choice for future airplane-mode sessions. If you want to ensure a radio is off, check its actual setting rather than relying only on the airplane icon.

16. Airplane mode on a tablet, laptop or smartwatch

Airplane mode is not limited to phones. Tablets and laptops with cellular hardware may offer a similar setting. A laptop without a cellular modem may still have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and its airplane-mode switch may disable those radios according to the operating system's design. A smartwatch may mirror a paired phone's airplane-mode setting or manage its own wireless connections.

For each device, check the manufacturer's support instructions. A tablet may have its own cellular eSIM or physical SIM, so switching off the phone does not automatically disable the tablet's mobile connection. A laptop connected to a phone hotspot may lose internet access when the phone's cellular data is disabled, even if the laptop's Wi-Fi remains on.

Airline rules can apply to all portable electronic devices, not only phones. Keep devices stowed when instructed, and follow rules about larger electronics, power banks and batteries. If the crew asks you to switch off a device completely, airplane mode is not a substitute.

17. Does airplane mode turn off emergency calls?

With cellular service disabled, the phone is not normally connected to the mobile network for an ordinary emergency call. Some phones and networks have special emergency-call behaviour, and availability depends on local law, network coverage, device capability and the situation. Do not rely on airplane mode as a way to preserve or guarantee emergency calling.

On an aircraft, the correct response to an emergency is to follow crew instructions and use the aircraft's established communication and safety procedures. A passenger phone is not a substitute for the aircraft's communication systems. If you need to contact emergency services before or after a flight, use the available permitted network and local emergency guidance.

18. What happens to notifications and background apps?

Apps that depend on cellular data may stop receiving live updates while the phone is offline. Email may not refresh, cloud files may not sync and social feeds may show cached content. Notifications that were already delivered remain visible, but new push notifications generally require an internet path such as Wi-Fi.

When you connect to aircraft Wi-Fi, some apps may resume background activity. Photos could upload, messages may synchronise and apps may download content. If the onboard service has a data allowance or paid package, this activity can use that allowance.

Before departure, decide which information should be available offline. Download boarding passes, maps, tickets, hotel details and entertainment. If you do not want large uploads or updates during a paid Wi-Fi session, pause automatic backups and app updates. Check the network's terms before using a hotspot or sharing access with other devices.

19. Does airplane mode disable alarms and timers?

Local alarms and timers generally continue to work because they are handled by the phone's clock and operating system rather than the mobile network. A downloaded song or offline playlist can also play without cellular service. However, a reminder that depends on a cloud service or a notification from an online account may not arrive until the phone reconnects.

If you rely on an alarm for a flight connection, use the built-in clock app and check its volume, sound and time zone settings. Do not rely only on a calendar notification that needs internet access. For an important event, consider a second independent reminder.

20. Common airplane-mode myths

Myth: Airplane mode switches off the entire phone

It does not. The phone stays on and local features such as the camera, alarm, notes and downloaded media can continue to work.

Myth: Wi-Fi can never be used in airplane mode

Many modern phones allow Wi-Fi to be turned on separately while airplane mode remains active. Whether you may use it during a flight depends on the airline's rules.

Myth: Bluetooth headphones always stop working

Bluetooth can often be re-enabled or remain available, depending on the phone. Follow airline instructions for accessories and device use.

Myth: Airplane mode is identical to switching off mobile data

Turning off mobile data stops cellular internet access but may leave the phone registered on the mobile network. Airplane mode disables the cellular connection more broadly.

Myth: Airplane mode guarantees zero charges of every kind

It prevents ordinary cellular roaming while cellular service remains disabled, but other services, separate devices or later changes to the settings may have different billing rules.

Myth: Airplane mode makes a phone completely invisible

It disables selected radios, not every possible signal or sensor. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth may be re-enabled, and location functions can behave differently by device.

Myth: You can ignore the crew if Wi-Fi works

No. Airline instructions and applicable aviation rules take priority over what a phone technically allows.

21. Troubleshooting: airplane mode will not turn off

If airplane mode remains enabled after you tap the control, try a simple sequence:

  1. Open the full Settings app and check the airplane-mode switch rather than relying only on the Quick Settings tile.
  2. Restart the phone if the setting appears stuck.
  3. Check whether a device-management policy, parental control or work profile is restricting network settings.
  4. Install available operating-system updates when you have a reliable connection and enough battery.
  5. If the cellular connection still does not return, check the SIM or eSIM status and the operator's service availability.
  6. Contact the device manufacturer or carrier if the control remains unavailable or the phone displays a persistent radio error.

Do not delete an eSIM profile as an early troubleshooting step. If the profile is removed, the operator may need to reissue it. For more help with mobile registration, see Google's general mobile connectivity troubleshooting guidance.

22. Troubleshooting: Wi-Fi or Bluetooth will not reconnect

If Wi-Fi or Bluetooth does not return after airplane mode is enabled, check each radio independently. Open Settings, verify that the connection is switched on and try connecting to a known network or paired accessory. A quick restart can refresh a stuck wireless state.

For Wi-Fi, confirm that the network name and password are correct and that the access point is working. Aircraft networks may require a portal login and may allow only one device per passenger or booking. For Bluetooth, check that the accessory has charge, is in pairing mode and is not already connected to another device.

If the Wi-Fi or Bluetooth controls are greyed out, check device policies and software updates. Do not assume the airplane-mode setting itself is faulty; a network problem, accessory issue or account restriction may be responsible.

23. A practical pre-flight checklist

  • Download boarding passes, tickets, maps and travel documents for offline access.
  • Download music, podcasts or videos before leaving a reliable connection.
  • Charge the phone and any wireless headphones.
  • Check the airline's current portable-device and Wi-Fi policy.
  • Turn on airplane mode when instructed by the airline.
  • Check that the cellular connection is disabled.
  • Enable Wi-Fi or Bluetooth only when the airline permits those connections.
  • Use the airline's official Wi-Fi network and follow its terms.
  • Keep calls and notifications silent where appropriate and respect other passengers.
  • Check roaming and dual-SIM settings before restoring cellular service after landing.

24. After landing: reconnect safely

Wait until the crew announces that device restrictions have ended or otherwise instructs passengers that cellular service may be used. Turn airplane mode off and allow the phone to register with the intended mobile network. If you are abroad, check the selected SIM, data line and roaming settings before opening apps that may use mobile data.

If you use a travel eSIM, make sure it remains selected for data if that is your plan. If you need your home number for calls or SMS, check that the home line is enabled and understand any roaming charges. Disable automatic data switching if you do not want the phone to move data to a different line unexpectedly.

If the phone does not reconnect, move to an area with coverage, restart the device or toggle airplane mode once after the aircraft has arrived and cellular use is permitted. Check that the SIM is active and that the operator has service in the destination. Do not change several network settings at once; test one change at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Does airplane mode turn off all signals?

No. It normally disables the cellular connection and may initially turn off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Those radios can often be enabled separately, depending on the phone and airline rules.

Can I use Wi-Fi with airplane mode on?

Many modern phones allow Wi-Fi to be switched on while airplane mode remains active. Use it only when the airline permits it and follow the onboard network terms.

Can I use Bluetooth headphones on a plane?

Often yes, when permitted by the airline. Bluetooth may need to be enabled separately after airplane mode is turned on.

Can I receive calls in airplane mode?

Ordinary cellular calls generally cannot reach the phone while its cellular radio is disabled. Internet-based calls may work over permitted Wi-Fi if the airline and service allow them.

Will I receive OTP SMS in airplane mode?

Traditional SMS normally will not arrive over the home mobile network while cellular service is disabled. Internet-based notifications may work over Wi-Fi if the service supports them.

Does airplane mode save battery?

It can reduce battery use when the phone would otherwise search for a weak mobile signal. The saving depends on which other features and radios remain active.

Does airplane mode stop roaming charges?

It prevents ordinary cellular roaming while cellular service stays disabled. If you turn cellular service back on, or use another line or service, charges may still apply under the relevant plan.

Does GPS work in airplane mode?

Satellite positioning may continue to work on some devices, but apps may lose network assistance, live traffic and map downloads. Download offline maps if needed.

Can I use WhatsApp in airplane mode?

WhatsApp and similar apps may work over permitted Wi-Fi. They cannot use cellular data while the cellular connection is disabled.

Will my alarm ring in airplane mode?

A locally stored alarm in the phone's clock app generally continues to work because it does not require a mobile network.

Can I turn airplane mode off during a flight?

Follow the airline's instructions. Do not turn it off simply because the phone allows it or because another flight had different rules.

Is airplane mode the same on iPhone and Android?

The purpose is similar, but the initial Wi-Fi and Bluetooth behaviour, menu labels and remembered settings can differ by model and software version.

Can I charge my phone in airplane mode?

Airplane mode does not prevent charging. Follow the airline's rules for charging ports, power banks and device use during the flight.

Should I use airplane mode on a cruise ship?

It can help prevent the phone from connecting to a ship's cellular network, which may have special charges. Check the ship's connectivity information and your operator's roaming terms.
